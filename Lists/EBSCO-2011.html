Abstract

  • The article presents brief descriptions of firms on the publication's list of the "100 Best Companies to Work For." Software maker SAS sits atop the rankings and is known for its generous fringe benefits including on-site health care and automobile cleaning. Second-ranked Boston Consulting Group has a reputation for paying well and encouraging its employees to do social work. Grocer Wegman's Food Markets came in third. It is regarded for its high level of customer service, and also for caring about the well-being of its workers.

THE LIST

01 HIRING

SAS

RANK LAST YEAR: 1

CARY, N.C.¹

EMPLOYEES²: 5,629

A 14-year veteran of this list, the software firm takes the top spot for the second year running. Its epic perks—on-site health care, summer camp for kids, car cleaning, a salon—almost make its massive gym seem like no big deal.

02 HIRING

BOSTON CONSULTING GROUP 8

BOSTON

EMPLOYEES: 1,713

The consulting giant not only avoided layoffs in the downturn but hired its largest class of recruits ever in 2010. They are drawn by generous pay and a commitment to social work: BCG took staff off projects to help in Haiti's quake.

03 HIRING

WEGMANS FOOD MARKETS 3

ROCHESTER, N.Y.

EMPLOYEES: 39,255

This customer-friendly supermarket chain cares about the well-being of its workers too. This year 11,000 took part in a challenge to eat five cups of fruit and vegetables and walk up to 10,000 steps a day for eight weeks.

04 HIRING

GOOGLE 4

MOUNTAIN VIEW, CALIF.

EMPLOYEES: 21,086 (WORLDWIDE)

With revenue up more than 20%, Google sweetened an already rich pot of perks by giving every employee a 10% pay hike. Googlers can also award one another $175 peer spot bonuses—last year more than two-thirds of them did so.

05 HIRING

NETAPP 7

SUNNYVALE, CALIF.

EMPLOYEES: 5,455

It was a rebound year for the data-storage firm (No. 1 in 2009), as revenues jumped 33% and it hired hundreds of new employees. Hourly executive assistants make $76,458 a year here, supplemented by a bonus of $21,917.

06 HIRING

ZAPPOS.COM 15

HENDERSON, NEV.

EMPLOYEES: 1,843

It's now part of Amazon.com, but the quirky, happy culture remains at the online shoe retailer: free lunches and free vending-machine fare, a life coach on hand, and "create fun and a little weirdness" as one of its guiding tenets.

07 HIRING

HOUSTON

08 HIRING

WOODLAND, CALIF.

09 HIRING

10 HIRING

11 HIRING

12 HIRING

EMPLOYEES: 2,884

13 HIRING

Methodology

To pick the 100 Best Companies to Work For, Fortune partners with the Great Place to Work Institute to conduct the most extensive employee survey in corporate America. Three hundred eleven companies participated in this year's survey. Most of a company's score (two-thirds) is based on the results of the Institute's Trust Index survey, which is sent to a random sample of employees from each company. The survey asks questions related to their attitudes about management's credibility, job satisfaction, and camaraderie. The other third of the scoring is based on the company's responses to the Institute's Culture Audit, which includes detailed questions about pay and benefit programs, and a series of open-ended questions about hiring practices, internal communication, training, recognition programs, and diversity efforts. Any company that is at least seven years old with more than 1,000 U.S. employees is eligible. The deadline for applying for next year's list is June 15, 2011.
