# Edmans (2011) Replication — List Audit & Sourcing Guide

## 1. What Edmans Uses

Edmans (2011) "Does the Stock Market Fully Value Intangibles?" uses the **"100 Best Companies to Work for in America"** list, which is **US-only**. The sample period is **April 1984 – December 2009** and the list comes from two distinct sources:

### A. Book Editions (pre-Fortune)
| List | Publication | Date | Source |
|------|------------|------|--------|
| 1984 | *The 100 Best Companies to Work for in America* (Levering, Moskowitz & Katz) | March 1984 | Book — portfolio formed April 1, 1984 |
| 1993 | *The 100 Best Companies to Work for in America*, 3rd Revised Ed. (Levering & Moskowitz) | February 1993 | Book — portfolio reformed March 1, 1993 |

### B. Fortune Magazine Annual Lists (1998–2009, and onward for out-of-sample)
Published annually, typically in the **January issue** of Fortune magazine (though in recent years publication has shifted to March/April).

| Year | Approx. Publication | Year | Approx. Publication |
|------|-------------------|------|-------------------|
| 1998 | January 12, 1998 | 2004 | January 2004 |
| 1999 | January 1999 | 2005 | January 2005 |
| 2000 | January 2000 | 2006 | January 2006 |
| 2001 | January 2001 | 2007 | January 22, 2007 |
| 2002 | January 2002 | 2008 | January 2008 |
| 2003 | January 2003 | 2009 | January/February 2009 |

For **out-of-sample extension** (2010–2025), the list continues annually. Recent years are published in March/April.

## 4. Where to Get Each Missing List

### A. The 1984 and 1993 Books (Highest Priority)

These are the hardest to source digitally. Options:

1. **Yale Library Physical Copy**: Search the Yale catalog for:
   - Levering, Robert; Moskowitz, Milton; Katz, Michael. *The 100 Best Companies to Work for in America.* Addison-Wesley, 1984. ISBN: 0201157748
   - Levering, Robert; Moskowitz, Milton. *The 100 Best Companies to Work for in America.* 3rd Revised Ed. Plume/Penguin, 1993. ISBN: 0452271231
   - Yale's Sterling Memorial Library or SML stacks likely has copies. You can also request via Borrow Direct or Interlibrary Loan (ILL).

2. **Google Books**: Partial previews may be available at:
   - https://books.google.com/books?id=QIeD7HQzI5kC (1984 edition)
   - https://books.google.com/books?id=rpsYAAAAIAAJ (1993 edition)
   - These usually don't show the full company list but are worth checking.

3. **AbeBooks / Amazon Used**: Copies are available for $5–$15 if you want your own.

4. **Contact Alex Edmans**: His data appendix or replication files may include the company lists. Check his website (alexedmans.com) or WRDS for replication data.

### B. Fortune Lists via EBSCO (1998–1999, 2008–2009, and other gaps)

You already used EBSCO for 2000–2006. The same approach works for the missing years:

1. **Go to**: Yale Library > Databases > Business Source Complete (or Business Source Ultimate)
   - Direct link: https://search.library.yale.edu/catalog/995667843408651
2. **Search for**: "100 Best Companies to Work For" in Fortune magazine
3. **Filter by date**: January issue of the relevant year
4. **The article title pattern** is typically: "The 100 Best Companies to Work For" or "100 Best Companies to Work For in America"

Specific EBSCO searches to run:
- Fortune, January 1998: "100 Best Companies" — this was the very first Fortune list
- Fortune, January 1999: same search
- Fortune, January/February 2008: same search
- Fortune, February 2009: same search

## 6. Important Notes for Replication

1. **US-only**: The Fortune/GPTW "100 Best Companies to Work For" is a **US list**. Great Place to Work also publishes "World's Best Workplaces" and regional lists — do NOT use those. The US list is sometimes subtitled "in America."

2. **Publication date matters**: Edmans forms portfolios the day after publication. You need to know the exact publication date for each year's list. For the Fortune era (1998+), it was typically the January issue (hitting newsstands in late December or early January). In recent years it shifted to March/April.

3. **CRSP matching**: After gathering all lists, you'll need to match company names to CRSP PERMNOs (or GVKEY for Compustat). This requires careful name matching since company names change over time. Edmans uses CRSP, not Compustat GVKEY, for his main analysis — so you'll want PERMNOs.

4. **Replication data**: Check if Edmans or WRDS has made the matched data available. This could save significant effort. His faculty page at London Business School or alexedmans.com may have replication files.
